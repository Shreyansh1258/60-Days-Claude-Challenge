# Day 51 – Product Discovery & Sprint Planning

## Overview

Day 51 marks the beginning of the 10-day capstone project. Instead of jumping directly into development, this stage focuses on structured product discovery and realistic planning.

Using Claude as a product discovery partner, I explored multiple project ideas through an interview-driven process. Rather than selecting the biggest idea, the discussion helped identify a project that could realistically be completed within ten days while still providing meaningful value.

The outcome was a clearly defined v1.0 scope, documented requirements, implementation roadmap, and project pitch—creating a strong foundation for the rest of the capstone.

---

## Objectives

- Discover the most practical capstone project idea
- Validate the project through guided questioning
- Define realistic MVP scope
- Identify features to include and exclude
- Establish measurable success criteria
- Create professional planning documents
- Build a development roadmap for the remaining days

---

## Deliverables

- Product Requirements Document (PRD)
- Implementation Blueprint
- Project Pitch Deck
- Approved Product Summary
- Product Discovery Interview
- Project Scope Definition
- Success Criteria

---

## Key Learnings

### Structured Discovery Produces Better Products

Instead of starting with assumptions, answering guided questions helped uncover the actual problem worth solving.

### Scope is More Important Than Features

A well-scoped product has a much higher chance of being completed successfully than an overambitious one.

### PRDs Reduce Development Confusion

Writing clear product requirements before coding creates alignment between the product vision and implementation.

### Success Criteria Should Be Defined Early

Knowing what "done" looks like makes future decisions significantly easier throughout development.

### Planning Saves Development Time

Investing time in discovery, documentation, and sprint planning minimizes uncertainty during implementation.

### AI Can Act as a Product Strategist

Claude effectively challenged assumptions, refined ideas, and helped transform rough concepts into a structured project plan.
